What is MLOps?

The discipline, the gap, and why it matters now more than ever

The Definition

MLOps (Machine Learning Operations) is the set of practices that reliably and efficiently move ML experiments into production and keep them healthy over time. It unifies ML engineering, data engineering, and platform engineering under a single operational discipline.

Why It Matters

90% of ML models never reach production. Of those that do, most degrade silently within months. MLOps is the engineering layer that collapses the gap between a notebook prototype and a system that serves real users at scale — reliably, safely, and economically.

The AI Era Shift

Foundation models changed the game. You no longer train from scratch for every task, but you now operate at massive scale — billions of parameters, petabytes of data, distributed clusters, RLHF pipelines, serving latency SLAs under 100 ms at millions of QPS.

Key insight: MLOps is not "DevOps for ML." It is an entirely new engineering discipline that subsumes DevOps and extends it with data-centric, experiment-centric, and model-centric concerns. A great MLOps engineer is part SRE, part data engineer, part distributed-systems engineer — and fluent in the ML research process.

Career Progression

What seniority level you'll operate at after completing each part

L3
Before the course — Junior / Mid SWE

You can write Python, train a model in a notebook, deploy a Flask app. MLOps is a black box. You've heard of Kubernetes but never operated it. You know cloud costs exist but not how to control them.

L4
After Part 1 (Ch01–05) — ML Infrastructure Engineer Part 1

You can provision cloud infrastructure with Terraform, containerize ML workloads, deploy Kubernetes clusters, and understand GPU/networking requirements. You can own the infrastructure layer for a small ML team.

Cloud architecture IaC (Terraform) Kubernetes GPU infrastructure
L5
After Part 2 (Ch06–10) — Senior ML Engineer Part 2

You can design and operate data pipelines, feature stores, and distributed training jobs. You can scale training from 1 GPU to hundreds with proper parallelism strategies. You evaluate and choose MLOps platforms appropriately. This is a competitive senior ML engineer.

Data pipelines Feature stores Distributed training Experiment tracking Ray / Spark
L5+
After Part 3 (Ch11–14) — Senior MLOps Engineer Part 3

You can build end-to-end ML pipelines with CI/CD automation, orchestrate complex workflows, and design high-performance model serving systems including LLM inference. You can lead the production readiness of an ML system independently.

ML pipelines CI/CD for ML Model serving (Triton/vLLM) Workflow orchestration
L6
After Part 4 (Ch15–16) — Staff MLOps Engineer Part 4

You can design comprehensive ML observability systems, detect and respond to model degradation, and operate LLM fine-tuning and RLHF pipelines. You can lead a team and define the MLOps strategy for an organization. This is competitive staff-level at most companies.

ML observability Drift detection RLHF pipelines LLM fine-tuning
L7
After Part 5 (Ch17–19) — Principal / MLOps Architect Part 5

You can reason about frontier AI infrastructure — from training clusters at the scale of GPT-4 and DeepSeek to production RAG and agent systems. You can make architectural decisions that balance cost, reliability, and capability at scale. You can architect MLOps systems for any organization from startup to hyperscaler.

Frontier model infra LLM system design RAG production systems Agent architecture Cost optimization at scale
A note on timelines

Reading alone won't get you there — you need to operate real systems. Pair this course with hands-on projects: set up a personal cluster, deploy a real model, debug a real failure. Engineers who combine this curriculum with production experience typically reach staff-level MLOps competency in 12–18 months. Those who are already working in adjacent roles (SRE, data engineering, ML research) often achieve this in 6–9 months.
