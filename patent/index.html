Why This Book

Law written in the language of technology

The patent system was not designed with transformer architectures, CRISPR gene editors, or diffusion models in mind. Yet these technologies now sit at the center of the most consequential patent disputes of our era. Most legal treatises assume a law-school audience; most engineering guides skip the doctrinal nuance that determines whether a claim actually holds up in litigation.

This book is different. Each chapter pairs doctrinal analysis with concrete examples drawn from real applications at the USPTO and EPO. The five case studies walk through complete claim strategies for landmark inventions — showing not only what was filed, but why, and what practitioners would do differently today.

How to Use This Book

Multiple paths through the material

Chapters 1–4 provide the doctrinal foundation. Chapters 5–9 are the practitioner's workbench. Chapters 10–12 address strategy and commercialization. The case studies can be read standalone or alongside the relevant core chapters.

For the ML or AI Engineer

Start with Chapter 2, then 5

Chapter 2 answers "can this even be patented?" — the threshold question for software and ML systems. Chapter 5 shows you how to draft claims that survive. Then read the Transformer and LoRA case studies to see real worked examples. Return to Chapter 1 for history and policy context when you need the "why" behind the rules.

For the Biotech Researcher

Start with Chapters 2, 4, and 6

Eligibility (Ch. 2) and specification requirements (Ch. 6) are the two biggest obstacles for life-science claims after Myriad and Amgen. Chapter 4 gives you the application anatomy. Then work through the CRISPR and AlphaFold case studies before tackling the international chapter (Ch. 11) — biotech patent strategy is almost always global from day one.

For Founders and IP Counsel

Start with Chapters 1, 3, and 10

Chapter 1 establishes the system-level framework. Chapter 3 on prior art and FTO is essential before any investment or product launch. Chapter 10 on portfolio strategy is the chapter VCs and acquirers wish founders had read before the term sheet. Round out with Chapter 12 on licensing and Chapter 11 on international coverage.

ℹ
A Note on Jurisdiction

This guide uses U.S. patent law (35 U.S.C., USPTO practice, Federal Circuit precedent) as its primary framework, with dedicated coverage of European Patent Convention practice, PCT procedure, and Chinese CNIPA practice in Chapters 8 and 11. Where doctrine differs materially across jurisdictions, differences are flagged inline.
